# S3 — Korean idol (아이돌)

## Definitions
- Idol: K-pop entertainer (group member or soloist) working under an agency's "highly managed star system".
- Trained in dance, vocals, foreign languages; a blend of visuals, music, fashion.
- "Parasocial kin": fans form familial bonds with idols.
- "Cultural technology": SM's label for its global audition/production method.
- "Wholesome image" required of trainees.

## Timeline
- ~1939 Jeogori Sisters debut (early vocal group).
- 1990s Lee Soo-man adopts trainee model from Japan's Johnny Kitagawa, inspired by New Kids on the Block.
- 2009 TVXQ challenges SM contract terms.
- 2018 onward 19 No.1 Billboard 200 albums by idol groups.

## Numbers
- SM receives ~300,000 applicants in nine countries a year.
- 2012 WSJ: training one Girls' Generation member cost US$3 million.
- 2013 avg idol annual earnings KR₩46.74m (~US$42,000), up from KR₩26.97m in 2010.
- Military service: men 18–35, 18 months.
- BTS and Stray Kids: six No.1 Billboard 200 albums each.

## Other
- Trainee period "indefinite" (months to years): vocal, dance, language classes, living together.
- Reports of pressure for plastic surgery and harsh weight limits.
- Dating bans common; decade-long contracts criticised.
- SM first to market "bands as brands".
- Fan activities: fan meetings, fan sites, fan cafés, KCON.
- Digest wording: "BTS first foreign group to perform at the Grammys" — ambiguous/likely mis-phrased (many non-US acts performed before); flagged, NOT used.
